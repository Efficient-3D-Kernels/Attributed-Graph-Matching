Fast-WCS: Fast Weighted Common Subgraph Matching via Level-3 BLAS Frank-Wolfe Optimization and Closed-Form SVD Superposition

We present an exact closed-form $\Theta(N^3)$ Frank-Wolfe homotopy continuation engine for high-dimensional relational matching under severe structural and spatial deformations. By eliminating Lawler tensor memory explosions via a Rank-1 co-matching factorization (Lemma 1), deriving an exact analytical gradient (Theorem 1), solving quartic line search in closed form (Theorem 3), and coupling topological correspondences with reflection-safe Kabsch 3D SVD superposition (Theorem 4), Fast-WCS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across four independent scientific domains while running up to $30\times$ faster than competing quadratic solvers.

Executive Problem Statement & Architectural Contributions

Given two weighted graphs $G$ and $H$ of sizes $M$ and $N$, and an integer $L \le \min(M, N)$, Weighted Common Subgraph (WCS) matching seeks the optimal partial permutation matrix $\mathbf{X} \in \Pi_{M, N, L}$ that minimizes structural and semantic mismatch. The problem is NP-hard, and prior continuous relaxations (such as Yang et al. 2018) suffered from three catastrophic limitations:

  1. Memory Explosion: Treating the co-matching indicator $\mathbf{U}$ as a dense matrix requires $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ tensor storage and high polynomial scaling ($\mathcal{O}(N^{5.4})$).
  2. Approximation Error: Relying on truncated gradients or heuristic line searches caused severe gradient inaccuracies and slow convergence.
  3. Symmetry Trapping: Pure topology solvers initialize at the flat uniform barycenter ($\nabla J_1 \propto \mathbf{1}$), leading to arbitrary tie-breaks and local saddle stagnation.

Our Unified Fast-WCS Framework: We address these limitations through four foundational innovations: (1) Lemma 1 proves that $\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{r}\mathbf{r}^T$ factors as a rank-one outer product, reducing memory to $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$; (2) Theorem 1 derives the exact analytical gradient in closed form; (3) Theorem 3 solves quartic line search via Cardano's cubic resolvent in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time; and (4) Theorems 4 & Lemma 2 provide domain-specialized adaptations that guarantee reflection-safe 3D alignment ($\det \mathbf{R} = +1$) and break scale-free community symmetries.
